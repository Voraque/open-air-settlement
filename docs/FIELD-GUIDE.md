# Field guide

## The first week

- **Day 1 — read the land.** Look for water, varied food, a defensible approach, and a route toward more than one biome. A permanent house can wait.
- **Days 2–3 — make night legible.** Light the approaches, create one entrance you can defend, and leave yourself sightlines.
- **Days 4–5 — cook on purpose.** Build a mixed plot. Farmer's Delight plus NutritionZ makes meal variety valuable; use JEI to solve a missing nutrient instead of living on bread.
- **Days 6–7 — choose a specialty.** One player can deepen food and habitat while the other surveys, fortifies, or makes storage work.

## The useful map

| Purpose | Mods | Why they matter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living world | Naturalist | Biome-specific animals and atmosphere. |
| Exploration | Terralith, Tectonic, Gliding, YUNG's Better Dungeons | Places worth crossing, vertical routes, and expeditions that need preparation. |
| Survival | NutritionZ, Farmer's Delight Refabricated, Zombie Awareness | Food variety stays relevant; zombies respond to blood, noise, and light. |
| Settlement | Supplementaries, Handcrafted, Traveler's Backpack | Rooms, signs, ropes, lights, useful details, and mobile supplies. |
| Late game | Tom's Simple Storage, CC: Tweaked, Oritech | Connected storage, programmable turtles, power, machines, and processing. |

## A shared-world ethic

Build visible logistics before hidden factories. Label turtle inputs and outputs; provide a stop switch for anything unattended. Let an automation remove the dull repeated work, not make the kitchen, farm, or fortification irrelevant.

## Things deliberately not included

- Waystones and a huge magic system: travel and settlement should matter first.
- Realistic Wildlife: it looked promising but its released implementation scanned the whole world every tick and made the server unusably slow. Naturalist remains.
- A mandatory shader: visuals should not prevent either player from joining.
